Istanbul in 24–48 Hours

Itinerary, airport transfer and costs · Last checked November 2026

Istanbul's greatest hits sit almost on top of each other: Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, the Basilica Cistern and Topkapı Palace are all within a few minutes' walk in Sultanahmet. Add the Grand Bazaar, a ferry across the water and a sunset over the Golden Horn, and one well-planned day covers a lot. Here's how to do it from the airport.

Prices below are in Turkish lira (TL) with rough euro equivalents at about 55 TL to €1 (late November 2026). Lira prices rise often, so treat them as a guide.

The M11 runs from about 06:00 to just after midnight, every 15 minutes or so. It does not run all night. You can tap a contactless bank card at the gates, or buy an Istanbulkart (about 165 TL), which also works on trams, buses and ferries.

Night arrival? The Sultanahmet bus (HVL-13) stops around midnight, but the Taksim bus (HVL-9) has a few departures through the night. Check the hava.ist timetable before you land, or take a taxi and insist on the meter.

From Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW)

SAW is on the Asian side. The M4 metro runs to Kadıköy (normal Istanbulkart fare), then take a ferry or the Marmaray train to the European side: 75–100 minutes in total to the old city. Havabüs coaches also run to Taksim and Kadıköy, but we couldn't confirm a current fare, so check on the day.

The perfect 24 hours in Istanbul

TimeWhat to do
08:30Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque) as it opens to visitors. Free. Use the south gate facing the square. It closes to tourists for about 60–90 minutes at each prayer time.
09:15Hagia Sophia, across the square. Foreign visitors pay €25 for the upper-gallery visit. Hours vary by season and it pauses for prayers, so check on the day.
10:30Basilica Cistern, 2 minutes away. A new operator took over in June 2026 and the current ticket price isn't confirmed. It was 1,950 TL (≈ €35) earlier in 2026, so check at the entrance.
11:30Topkapı Palace, the Ottoman sultans' palace. 2,750 TL (≈ €50) including the Harem and Hagia Irene. Open 09:00–17:00, closed Tuesdays. Allow 2–3 hours.
14:30Late lunch on Divanyolu, then the Grand Bazaar. Free to enter. Open Monday to Saturday, roughly 08:30/09:00 to 19:00. Closed Sundays and on religious holidays.
16:30Walk up to the Süleymaniye Mosque for the view over the Golden Horn (visit outside prayer times), then down through the market streets to the Spice Bazaar and Eminönü.
SunsetTake a public ferry from Eminönü across the water. Fares are around 53–65 TL on an Istanbulkart, depending on the route. It's the cheapest Bosphorus view there is.
EveningGalata Tower (€30, open until 23:00, last entry 22:00), then dinner in Karaköy or around Taksim.
Mosque dress code: shoulders and knees covered for everyone. Women cover their hair (scarves are handed out free at the Blue Mosque). Shoes come off in prayer areas, and a bag is provided.
Friday tip: the Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia close to visitors around the midday Friday prayer and reopen in the early afternoon. On a Friday, do Topkapı Palace at midday and the mosques in the afternoon.

With a second day

  • Cross to Asia: ferry to Kadıköy for the food market streets, or to Üsküdar (about 20 minutes from Eminönü) for waterfront mosques and tea by the water.
  • Bosphorus by ferry: the public ferries up the Bosphorus are far cheaper than tour boats. Check the Şehir Hatları timetable, as winter sailings are fewer.
  • Balat and Fener: colourful old streets along the Golden Horn, good for an unhurried morning walk.
  • İstiklal Avenue from Taksim down to Galata for shopping, street food and the old tram.

Is a layover in Istanbul worth it?

Time between flightsAdvice
Under 8 hoursStay at the airport. With 1–1.5 hours each way to Sultanahmet, you'd get very little time in the city.
8–12 hoursGo. Metro to Sultanahmet, then the Blue Mosque, Hagia Sophia and one more sight, a meal, and back.
12–24 hoursFollow the 24-hour plan, trimmed to fit. Skip Topkapı if you're short of time.
OvernightSleep in Sultanahmet or Karaköy and start at the Blue Mosque at 08:30.
Flying Turkish Airlines? Its free Touristanbul tour is for passengers with a 6–24 hour layover at Istanbul Airport on a Turkish Airlines ticket (ticket number starting 235). Register at the desk in the arrivals hall at least 30 minutes before the tour starts. The Bosphorus tour runs only from April to October. Check the airline's Touristanbul page for current tour times.

Aim to be back at Istanbul Airport about 3 hours before departure, because it's huge and traffic is unpredictable. Check whether your passport needs a visa or e-Visa before you leave the terminal.

Where to eat

Pork is not part of Turkish cuisine and mainstream meat is generally halal, but alcohol is served in many restaurants, especially around Taksim and Beyoğlu. The old city (Fatih, Sultanahmet) and Üsküdar have the most alcohol-free places. Street food is excellent and cheap: a simit (sesame bread ring) costs about 15–25 TL, a döner wrap 150–250 TL, and a lokanta (canteen) lunch 350–650 TL.

Quick answers

How long does it take to get from Istanbul Airport to Sultanahmet?

About 60–80 minutes by M11 metro, M2 and the T1 tram, around 110 minutes on the Havaist HVL-13 bus, or 50–105 minutes by taxi, depending on traffic.

How much is the metro from Istanbul Airport?

One normal Istanbulkart fare per ride, about 42–46 TL in late 2026. Reaching Sultanahmet takes three rides, so about 130–140 TL. You can tap a contactless bank card instead of buying a card.

Is a layover in Istanbul worth it?

With 8 hours or more between flights, yes. With less, stay airside: the airport is 1–1.5 hours from the old city each way.

How much does Hagia Sophia cost?

€25 for foreign visitors, for the upper-gallery visitor route. The Blue Mosque next door is free.

What day is Topkapı Palace closed?

Tuesdays. The Grand Bazaar is closed on Sundays, so plan around both.

Is food in Istanbul halal?

Mainstream Turkish meat is generally halal and pork isn't used in Turkish cooking. Many restaurants serve alcohol, though, so ask if that matters to you.

Athens in 24–48 Hours

Itinerary, airport transfer and costs · Last checked November 2026

Athens rewards a short stay better than almost any capital: the Acropolis, the ancient Agora, the old town of Plaka and the best sunset hills are all within walking distance of each other. Here's how to fit the best of it into one or two days, starting from the airport.

Normal city tickets aren't valid on the airport section of the metro: buy an airport ticket, or tap a contactless card or phone. Trains run roughly every 30–36 minutes from about 06:00 to 23:30.

Heads-up: Line 3 has had periods of track works in 2026. If you see a works notice, the X95 bus or a taxi is the safer option.

The perfect 24 hours in Athens

TimeWhat to do
08:00Acropolis at opening. Timed entry is mandatory, so book your slot in advance on the official site. €30 adult, the same all year. Allow 1.5–2 hours.
10:30Acropolis Museum, 5 minutes away. A separate ticket of about €15–20. Don't miss the top-floor Parthenon Gallery.
12:30Lunch, then the lanes of Plaka and the hidden whitewashed village of Anafiotika.
14:30Ancient Agora and the Temple of Hephaestus (€20). Winter hours can be shorter, so check on the day.
16:30Monastiraki Square and the flea market.
SunsetPhilopappos Hill (free) or Lycabettus Hill for the view over the whole city.
EveningDinner in Psirri or Plaka.
Did you know? The six Caryatids holding up the Erechtheion porch are exact copies. Five of the originals are indoors at the Acropolis Museum (the sixth is in the British Museum), so see the hill first and the real statues straight after.
Insider tip: use the South Slope entrance. Instead of the main west gate, enter at the South Slope entrance on Dionysiou Areopagitou, opposite the Acropolis Museum and a short walk from Akropoli metro. It is usually much quieter, your normal Acropolis ticket covers it, and the walk up passes the Theatre of Dionysus, the Sanctuary of Asklepios and a viewpoint over the Odeon of Herodes Atticus.

With a second day

  • Syntagma Square: the Changing of the Guard happens on the hour (free), followed by a walk through the National Garden.
  • Panathenaic Stadium, home of the first modern Olympics, then the Temple of Olympian Zeus and Hadrian's Arch.
  • Varvakios Central Market for lunch. The Omonia area next to it has the city's largest cluster of halal restaurants.
  • National Archaeological Museum or quiet, underrated Kerameikos in the afternoon.

Aim to be back at the airport 2–2.5 hours before departure, and check whether your passport needs a Schengen visa before you leave the terminal.

Where to eat

Souvlaki and gyros are everywhere and cheap (€4–6), but most mainstream shops use pork by default, so ask for chicken (kotopoulo) if needed. Grilled-fish tavernas are a great fallback.

Quick answers

How long does it take to get from Athens airport to the city?

About 40 minutes by metro (Line 3) to Syntagma or Monastiraki, 60–90 minutes by the X95 bus, or 35–50 minutes by taxi depending on traffic.

How much is the metro from Athens airport?

€9 one way or €16 return (valid 30 days). Normal city tickets don't work on the airport section, but you can tap a contactless card or phone.

Is a layover in Athens worth it?

With 7 hours or more between flights, yes: the Acropolis area is about 40 minutes from the airport by metro. With less than 7 hours, stay at the airport.

Do I need to book the Acropolis in advance?

Yes. Entry is by timed slot, so book on the official site before you go. The 08:00 slot is the quietest and coolest.

Where can I find halal food in Athens?

The Omonia area, especially Menandrou and Acharnon streets, has the city's largest cluster of halal restaurants. Mainstream gyros shops usually use pork, so ask for chicken.
